# Who uses workout apps, and why they stay or leave

Research for deciding which LogIt personas matter most. Compiled 2026-11-21.

How to read this:

- Every claim has its source inline. **Strong** means peer-reviewed, an official store page, or a company's own published data. **Medium** means a vendor benchmark or company marketing data with a stated method. **Weak** means a blog or a summary without a method, or a source that repeats someone else's numbers.
- Section 3 includes a small analysis of my own: about 17,000 recent Google Play reviews of Hevy, Strong, Fitbod and JEFIT, sorted by theme using keywords. The method and its limits are given there.

---

## Summary

| Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|
| Most people who try a health/fitness app stop soon. About 28% come back on day 1, 18% on day 7 and 8.5% on day 30. A median of 70% stop within 100 days. | Medium (benchmark) / Strong (review) |
| The first 4 weeks decide who stays. In a peer-reviewed study of about 390,000 Fitbod users, consistency in the first 28 days was the strongest predictor of sticking with it. Only about 10% of beginners were still training a year later, against 26% of advanced users. | Strong |
| Beginners are a large share of new users. Fitbod says 60% of its 2.2 million new accounts in 2025 were new to strength training. | Medium (company data) |
| A paywall that appears after setup is the biggest single reason for angry reviews of guided apps. In 70% of Fitbod's 1–2★ reviews it is about price or paywall, and most of its "left on day one" reviews are about a paywall that appeared after setup. | Medium (my review count) |
| Trackers leave when an update breaks logging or loses their history, more than over price. In JEFIT's 1–2★ reviews, 28% are about an update or redesign. Strong's 2025 sign-in change produced a wave of "lost years of data" reviews that name Hevy as the place they went. | Medium (my review count) |
| About 1 in 4 US gym members used a personal trainer in 2024, and 1 in 3 did small-group training. | Medium–strong (industry association) |

### What I take from this

- The largest groups are **people who don't track or track on paper** (about 40% plus 20%), and **beginners**, who make up the majority of new installs in a guided app.
- **Experienced lifters are a minority of downloads but a big minority of people who stay.** In the Fitbod study, advanced users are 24% of week-one survivors and more than twice as likely to still be training at a year.
- **Coached clients are common in gyms** (about 1 in 4 US members used a PT), but most of that coaching is in person. No data says how many of those clients use an app the coach chose.

## 2. Why people stop in the first day, week and month

### Retention curves

| Measure | Number | Source and confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Health & Fitness day 1 / 7 / 30 retention | 28% / 18.1% / 8.5% (all apps: 28.3% / 17.9% / 7.9%) | OneSignal, [Mobile App Benchmarks 2024](https://onesignal.com/mobile-app-benchmarks-2024). Medium: a vendor benchmark with no method stated on the page. Other vendors report a day-30 figure as low as about 3% (summarised in [Business of Apps, Health & Fitness benchmarks](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks); weak, secondary). |
| Share who stop within 100 days | Median 70%. Most drop-off comes "soon after acquisition", then it slows. Physical-activity apps last longer than alcohol or smoking apps. | Kidman et al., JMIR 2024, [When and Why Adults Abandon Lifestyle Behavior and Mental Health Mobile Apps: Scoping Review](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897) (18 studies, 525,824 participants). Strong. |
| Beginners in a strength app over a year | Median time to dropout was 19 weeks, with the steepest fall between weeks 10 and 15. 10.1% of beginners, 18.3% of intermediates and 25.8% of advanced users were still adherent at 52 weeks. | [Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 2026, Fitbod users](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1855668/full). Strong, but it only covers people who trained every week in their first 28 days. |
| What predicts staying | More active days in the first 28 days: each standard deviation more meant a 27% lower dropout hazard at week 11. More equipment variety and a higher share of strength work also helped. Age and sex mattered less than behaviour. | Same study. Strong (observational, so not proof of cause). Fitbod's own report agrees: people who trained consistently in their first 3 months set 3.6× more PRs ([State of Strength 2025](https://fitbod.me/blog/fitbod-2025-state-of-strength-report/), medium). |
| When paying decisions happen | 82% of trial starts happen on the day of install. Nearly 30% of annual subscriptions are cancelled in the first month. Health & Fitness has a median trial-to-paid rate of 39.9% and a 4.71% refund rate (second highest of any category). | RevenueCat, [State of Subscription Apps 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025) (about 75,000 apps). Medium–strong: company data with a large sample. |
| Hard paywall vs freemium | 12.1% vs 2.2% download-to-paid by day 35, but 5.8% vs 3.4% refunds | Same source. The hard paywall makes more money from fewer, more committed people, at the cost of losing everyone else on day 0. |

**Takeaway:** day-one loss comes from cost surprises and confusion. Week-to-month loss comes from logging effort and fading motivation. Privacy is a real reason for some people, but rarely the loudest one.

## 3. What overwhelms beginners, and what well-reviewed apps do first

### My review analysis (method)

- I pulled the newest English (US) Google Play reviews with the open-source `google-play-scraper` package on 2026-11-21, kept those from 2024 onward, and matched keywords for each theme. Each review can count toward several themes.
- Samples:
  - Hevy: 11,999 reviews (Dec 2024 to Nov 2026), 298 of them 1–2★.
  - Strong: 833 reviews, 213 of them 1–2★.
  - Fitbod: 2,032 reviews, 633 of them 1–2★.
  - JEFIT: 2,150 reviews, 584 of them 1–2★.
- Limits: keyword matching is crude and over-counts words like "free" and "pro". The figures are rough shares, not exact measurements. Hevy's very low negative share (2.5%) probably also reflects how it asks people to leave reviews. **Confidence: medium.**

Share of 1–2★ reviews that mention each theme:

| Theme | Hevy | Strong | Fitbod | JEFIT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price, paywall, subscription | 27% | 23% | **70%** | 30% |
| Bugs, crashes, lost data, sync | 20% | 17% | 6% | 17% |
| An update or redesign made it worse | 9% | 20% | 3% | **28%** |
| Account, sign-in, login | 8% | **21%** | 7% | 7% |
| AI or suggestions were wrong | 4% | 4% | 12% | 9% |
| Guidance, plan, beginner | 5% | 4% | 6% | 10% |
| Confusing, cluttered, overwhelming | 5% | 5% | 1% | 7% |
| Ads | 1% | 0% | 4% | 5% |
| Import, export, "my data" | 2% | 3% | 1% | 2% |

Reviews that describe leaving on day one (uninstalled straight away, couldn't get past setup, and so on) were 9% of Fitbod's negative reviews, and **78% of those were about a paywall**. For JEFIT the figures were 6% and 22%, for Hevy 4% and 45%, and for Strong 3% and 14%.

### What beginners complain about (with the most-upvoted examples)

1. **A paywall after setup, the "bait and switch".** This is the clearest pattern. Fitbod lets people answer the questions, build a plan and make an account, and only then asks for payment details:
   - "making the user set up the app + personalize the available equipment + create and account and AFTER THAT you realize that you can't even start the trial without adding payment info… Made me lose 20 minutes. Uninsta[lled]"
   - "Downloaded it, filled out the biometrics, etc only to be slapped with an enormous pay wall before I could even see my workout"
   - "only 3 workouts before you HAVE to pay"

   One Hevy review complains of a subscription screen "from the very beginning". Its free tier is generous, but a hard-looking first screen still costs it people. Source: [Fitbod Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fitbod.fitbod) and [Hevy Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hevy) reviews, via my scrape.
2. **Forced sign-up before seeing anything.** "I will never understand why apps force users to sign up or give their email first before they can even SEE, touch, experiment with… the app" (Strong, 2★). JEFIT: "the app forces you to register and ask lot of personal questions before you can use it."
3. **Clutter and features you can't turn off.** JEFIT: "They keep adding features and forcing you to go through them without being able to turn it off. I just want a simple goddamn workout tracker." Also: "cluttered by useless ai-driven 'insights'… they'll pop up every time you record a set."
4. **Generated workouts that don't fit the person.** Fitbod (123 upvotes): "the second one was way too easy and the third had things I simply can't do… I am not capable of doing push ups." Onboarding questions that felt off also put people off. JEFIT: "They ask your 'build' But only go from skinny, average to overweight."
5. **Free-tier limits found mid-use.** Hevy: "Doesn't allow you to add more than 4 custom workouts in free plan and does not even bother telling you." Strong caps free users at 3 custom routines ([Strong App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id464254577)). StrongLifts dropped its free tier altogether in 2025 ([StrongLifts support: What happened to Power Pack?](https://support.stronglifts.com/article/148-power-pack)).

Surprisingly, "too many options" as such is a small theme (1–7% of negative reviews). Beginners complain much more about **being blocked**, by a paywall, an account wall or a wrong workout, than about being overwhelmed. Clutter complaints come mostly from long-time users after a redesign.

### What the well-reviewed ones do in the first minutes

I couldn't find a published, method-backed study of onboarding in these apps (see Gaps). From the store listings and positive reviews:

- **Hevy (4.9★):**
  - The listing leads with "No ads and free", "simplest, most intuitive", unlimited workouts, exercise videos and ready-made routines ([Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hevy)).
  - In its 4–5★ reviews, 24% mention simple, easy or intuitive, 14% mention free, and 39% mention tracking, progress or graphs.
  - Beginners praise ready-made routines and looping exercise clips: "this beginner feels much more equipped stepping into the gym now… each exercise has a looping clip to show you the correct form" and "Unlike other apps I don't find myself having to scroll through a lot of fluff."
- **Fitbod:** the beginners who stay value being told what to do and what weight to use: "Extremely handy for a beginner with no idea what to do!" and "given me the confidence at the gym… how much I should be lifting." Its 4–5★ reviews mention guidance (11%) and AI or suggestions (15%) more than any other app does. So guidance works, and it's the paywall timing that hurts Fitbod.
- **Strong:** "Simplest interface of any fitness app." It is built for "progressive barbell routines" and promises recording "as effortlessly and as quickly as possible" ([Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.strongapp.strong)).

**Pattern:** the winners let you **see and start a real workout before any wall**, keep the logging screen plain, and show how each exercise is done.

## 4. What makes trackers and experienced lifters switch

| Trigger | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **An update breaks logging or the layout** | 28% of JEFIT's 1–2★ reviews: "The new UI update has made it useless… I just spent more time trying to edit my weight and rep inputs than actually exercising"; "Why does every update seem to make this app WORSE?… God forbid you should find a station busy at the gym and have to rearrange your plan"; "time to move to a different app". | Medium (my scrape) |
| **Losing history, often because of account changes** | Strong's early-2025 sign-in change produced a cluster of reviews (21% of its negative reviews mention account or login): "Got signed out… when I signed back into my account it was empty. Lost over 800 workouts"; "3 years worth of data gone… I recommend getting the app, HEVY instead"; "had been using this app for multiple years without an account. they recently seem to have enforced having an account but once I created one all my previous workout data was lost." JEFIT: "All the data from my 10 years plus using this app is gone!!" | Medium. Strong's own release notes mention fixes for "sign-in sessions that could be lost" (summarised via search; I didn't read them directly). |
| **Features moved behind a paywall, or a free tier removed** | JEFIT: "consistently made it more and more restrictive putting more and more features behind paywalls." Strong: "Body measurements behind a paywall." A Pro user: "It looks now I am no longer a pro member and cannot create custom workout templates… I'm going to delete this app." StrongLifts went Pro-only in 2025 ([support page](https://support.stronglifts.com/article/148-power-pack)), and a Hevy reviewer said they switched "when SL became a fully paid app." | Strong for the StrongLifts policy; medium for the reviews |
| **Abandonware** | Strong: "Been subscribed for well over 2 years and now moved to an app which doesn't feel like abandonware." | Medium–weak (a single review) |
| **Import makes switching cheap** | Hevy has a documented "Import Strong CSV" path, but routines don't transfer ([Hevy help: How to Import Strong App CSV Files](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/38001424401943-How-to-Import-Strong-App-CSV-Files-and-Export-Your-Data-in-Hevy)). Strong advertises "Export all of your data… in CSV format" ([Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.strongapp.strong)). Several smaller apps market Strong/Hevy CSV import. | Strong (official docs) |
| **Ads** | A minor issue for this category: 0–5% of negative reviews. The leaders (Hevy, Strong, Fitbod) show none. | Medium |
| **Logging speed** | Rarely named outright (2–3% of negative reviews). It shows up instead as "too many taps after the redesign" complaints. Vendors such as Setgraph market themselves on fewer taps than Strong ([setgraph.app](https://setgraph.app/articles/best-strong-app-alternatives-(2025)); **weak**, vendor). | Medium–weak |
| **Data ownership and privacy as a reason to switch** | Little direct evidence. Privacy is about 2% of complaints in consumer health app reviews ([Uncovska 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00862-3)), but 29% of people who stopped using a health app disliked data being shared with friends ([Krebs 2015](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2015/4/e101/)). In practice, "ownership" shows up as **fear of losing history** and **wanting export**, not as privacy talk. | Medium |

In short, experienced lifters rarely leave for a missing feature. They leave when the app **gets slower or less reliable at the one thing they use daily**, or when **their history is at risk**. They go to whichever app imports that history.

## 5. What group-class coaches use today (brief)

No independent survey exists. These are vendor descriptions, so the confidence is weak to medium:

- **CrossFit and functional-fitness boxes:**
  - Gym software with a daily "whiteboard" or WOD: SugarWOD (about 4,000 gyms and 900,000 athletes, by its own claim; [sugarwod.com](https://sugarwod.com/?p=8626)), Wodify ("5,000+ gyms"; [Wodify FAQs](https://www.wodify.com/blog/wodify-faqs)), PushPress, and Beyond the Whiteboard.
  - Members log their own score against the shared workout.
- **Small-group PT studios:**
  - Coaching platforms with a "group" or "live program" feature that gives everyone the same session: CoachRx ([Using CoachRx for group fitness gyms](https://intercom.help/coachrx/en/articles/7885597-using-coachrx-for-group-fitness-gyms)) and Trainerize groups.
  - TV display: Exercise.com ([Display TV workouts](https://www.exercise.com/platform/tv-workouts/)), TeamBuildr TV modules ([teambuildr.com](https://teambuildr.com/tv-modules-features)), PEAR ([Studios & Fitness Facilities](https://pearhealthlabs.com/solutions/studios-and-fitness-facilities/)), Bridge Athletic's "weight room mode".
  - Trainerize users have asked for a TV or timer display that it doesn't have ([ABC Trainerize ideas forum](https://ideas.abcfitness.com/forums/167887-coach-trainer-abc-trainerize/suggestions/40599877-ability-to-show-workouts-videos-and-timer-on-a-tv)).
- **The rest:** a physical whiteboard, paper sheets, or a TV showing the timer, with booking in Mindbody, Glofox and similar tools. Anecdotal; I found no survey data.
- **Market signal:** 32.3% of US gym members did small-group training in 2024 ([HFA](https://www.healthandfitness.org/how-77-million-fitness-members-work-out-new-hfa-data-reveals-shifting-equipment-training-and-membership-trends/)). Hevy Coach, the closest rival to LogIt Studio, lists no group or class feature ([pricing page](https://hevycoach.com/pricing/)).

The common pattern: **one workout on a shared screen, and each member's weights recorded somewhere**, either by the member on their phone or by nobody.

## What this means for LogIt's personas

### P0: App-store shopper (a beginner comparing several free apps)

- **How common:** the largest group by downloads. About 60% of new accounts in a guided app are new to lifting ([Fitbod 2025](https://fitbod.me/blog/fitbod-2025-state-of-strength-report/)), and 21.5% of people who left an app say they downloaded several and kept one ([Mustafa 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8872344/)). Only about 28% return on day 1 ([OneSignal](https://onesignal.com/mobile-app-benchmarks-2024)).
- **Stays if** they see and start a real workout fast, nothing blocks them, and it looks simple and free.
- **Leaves if** a paywall or account wall shows up after they've invested effort, the questions feel off, or the first workout doesn't fit them.
- **Test hardest:**
  1. Time from install to the first logged set, with **no account and no payment screen** on the way. Count taps and seconds.
  2. Any Pro or trial prompt in the first session: does it ever appear after setup effort? Is it obvious what Free keeps?
  3. Whether the first suggested workout is doable for a true beginner: no push-up wall, home equipment respected, an easy way to swap.

### P1: Beginner who follows a plan

- **How common:** most of the P0 people who survive day 1. In the Fitbod data, beginners are about 26% of week-one survivors, and only about 10% are still going at a year ([Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1855668/full)).
- **Stays if** they train often in the first 28 days, see progress and get guidance on weights.
- **Leaves if** motivation fades (31.6–40.5% of people who stop give that reason), logging feels like a chore (44.5%), or the plan is too easy or too hard.
- **Test hardest:**
  1. The week 1–4 loop: are missed days absorbed without guilt, is the next workout obvious, are weight suggestions sensible?
  2. Logging effort per set on a phone, mid-workout.
  3. The moment the plan should grow (T2): rep ranges and load stepping up, not stalling.

### P3/P4: Experienced lifter and competitor

- **How common:** a minority of downloads, but 24% of active users in the Fitbod data and the most likely to still be around at a year (25.8%). Competitors are a small slice (low hundreds of thousands worldwide; weak data).
- **Stays if** logging is fast and stable, their history is safe, and their own programs (5/3/1 and the like) fit.
- **Leaves if** a redesign slows logging, data is lost after an account or sync change, or features move behind a paywall.
- **Test hardest:**
  1. Strong and Hevy CSV import: full history, units, and what happens to routines. Hevy's import drops routines, so carrying them over is an edge for LogIt.
  2. Editing during and after a workout: reordering when a station is busy, changing a past set, all fast.
  3. Account and sync edge cases, such as signing in on an existing offline profile, a new phone, or a server move. History must never vanish (Strong's 2025 lesson).

### P5: Just tracks workouts

- **How common:** large. 28% of gym-goers use a fitness app, and roughly 3 in 10 track in a notebook, phone notes or a spreadsheet ([YouGov 2022](https://yougov.com/articles/43857-us-britain-tools-gym-goers-use-track-fitness)). These people are switchers from paper and from Strong, Hevy or JEFIT.
- **Stays if** it's plain and fast, with no ads, no forced features, and export available.
- **Leaves if** they hit clutter they can't turn off ("I just want a simple goddamn workout tracker"), lose data, or meet surprise free-tier caps (Hevy 4 routines, Strong 3).
- **Test hardest:**
  1. With suggestions off, the app never nudges, pops up "insights" or shows coaching UI mid-set.
  2. Offline use, then export, backup and restore round-trips with nothing lost.
  3. What Free allows: no routine cap surprise, and any limit stated up front.

### P8–P11: Coaches and their clients (free invite, paid elsewhere, Stripe)

- **How common:** about 1 in 4 US gym members used a PT in 2024 ([HFA](https://www.healthandfitness.org/how-77-million-fitness-members-work-out-new-hfa-data-reveals-shifting-equipment-training-and-membership-trends/)). No good data on how many coaches use an app. The closest rival, Hevy Coach, charges $25/month for up to 10 clients, with free client apps ([pricing](https://hevycoach.com/pricing/)).
- **Coach stays if** setting up is quick, sample clients make sense and assigning a program is easy. **Client stays if** joining is one link, they never pay twice, and the program is simply "today's workout".
- **They leave if** the client hits a paywall or account friction (the same P0 issues apply to invited clients), or payment states get confusing.
- **Test hardest:**
  1. A client joining from an invite link on a fresh phone: account, program shown, first workout logged, never asked to buy Pro.
  2. The payment paths (free, paid elsewhere, Stripe paid, then failed, then fixed, then stopped), with what the client sees at each step.
  3. A coach going from zero to the first program assigned, and the client's own history kept when they join or leave a coach.

### P15: Group-class PT (not catered for yet)

- **How common:** the demand side is big (32.3% of US members did small-group training). The supply side is crowded at gym level (SugarWOD, Wodify, PushPress) but thin for a single coach with about 10 people.
- **What they need,** going by what exists: one live workout on a screen or the coach's phone, with each person's weights recorded and carried forward.
- **Test hardest (once built):**
  1. Running one session for 10 people from one phone.
  2. Letting members log their own sets, or not.
  3. Carrying each member's loads into next week.

  Until then, check that a coach can at least assign the same program to many clients quickly.

## Gaps and confidence

- **No reliable split of downloads by persona.** The only experience-level data is Fitbod's (a self-reported, survivor-filtered sample, from an app aimed at guidance). Tracker-first apps like Strong and Hevy publish no user breakdown. Treat P0 and P1 as "most downloads" and P3/P4 as "a minority who stay" (medium confidence).
- **No day-1/day-7 retention specific to strength apps.** Only category benchmarks from vendors, and they disagree (day-30 ranges from about 3% to 8.5%).
- **No peer-reviewed analysis of strength-app store reviews.** Section 3 is my own keyword count of Google Play reviews: one store, English, US only, newest reviews only. No App Store reviews and no Reddit (Reddit blocked automated access, and the blog summaries of Reddit I found were vendor marketing).
- **No published onboarding teardown with data** (time to first workout, drop-off per screen) for Hevy, Strong, Fitbod or JEFIT. What I say about first minutes comes from store listings and reviews.
- **Personal-trainer software use:** no survey of which tools PTs or group-class coaches use. Section 5 is vendor claims.
- **Powerlifting numbers** come from blogs analysing OpenPowerlifting. They are weak and disagree with each other (130,000 vs 262,000 at the 2019 peak).
- **Several sources are dated.** Krebs & Duncan is from 2015 and YouGov from 2022. Both are still the best I found for their specific numbers.
- **Not checked:** iOS-only apps, Europe-specific data (most data is US), and whether privacy-first positioning wins downloads. I found no evidence either way, beyond privacy rarely appearing in complaints.
